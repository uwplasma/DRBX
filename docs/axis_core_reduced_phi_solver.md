# Reduced Cartesian-core formulation for the axis-regular φ solve

This document specifies a long-term linear-solver formulation in which the
axis-regular core is represented by Cartesian coefficients rather than by
independent polar cell values. The intended unknown is

\[
y = (c_{\mathrm{core}},\,\phi_{\mathrm{outer}}),
\]

and the full polar field used by the existing operator is recovered as
\(\phi = T y\). The formulation is designed for the perpendicular elliptic
solve for \(\phi\), but the same construction can be used for any operator
whose domain must satisfy the same axis regularity constraint.

## 1. Full polar space and core observations

Let \(X \cong \mathbb{R}^{N}\) be the full discrete polar space. A vector
\(f\in X\) contains all active polar cells, including the cells in the
axis-core rings, with the repository's chosen ordering over radial, poloidal,
and toroidal coordinates. Let

\[
M = \operatorname{diag}(m_1,\ldots,m_N), \qquad m_i>0,
\]

be the diagonal cell-volume mass matrix. The full discrete inner product and
norm are

\[
\langle f,g\rangle_M = f^T M g,
\qquad
\|f\|_M^2 = f^T M f.
\]

The discussion applies plane-by-plane in the toroidal coordinate. For
clarity, the notation below describes one \(\eta\) plane; stacking all
planes gives the full three-dimensional construction. Let \(K\) be the set
of core observation values used to fit the Cartesian expansion. In the
current design, \(K\) consists of all poloidal cells in the configured core
observation rings, with those rings fully active. Let \(O\) be the remaining
outer polar cells. Thus

\[
X = X_K \oplus X_O,
\qquad
f=(f_K,f_O).
\]

Write \(M_K\) and \(M_O\) for the corresponding restrictions of \(M\).
The number of core observations is \(n_K=|K|\). The core representation has
\(p\) Cartesian coefficients. For a total Cartesian polynomial degree \(d\),

\[
p = \#\{(a,b):a+b\le d\}
  = \frac{(d+1)(d+2)}{2}.
\]

For the current degree-three setup, \(p=10\). The coefficient vector
contains the coefficients of

\[
q(x,y)=\sum_{a+b\le d}c_{ab}x^a y^b.
\]

No poloidal Fourier transform is part of this representation. The Cartesian
polynomial contains the compatible low-order angular behavior implicitly.

## 2. Cartesian design matrix and mass-weighted restriction

Let

\[
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n_K\times p}
\]

be the Cartesian design matrix evaluated at the core observation locations:

\[
(Vc)_i = q_c(x_i,y_i), \qquad i\in K.
\]

Assume that \(V\) has full column rank. Define the core Gram matrix

\[
G = V^T M_K V.
\]

Because \(M_K\) is positive definite and \(V\) has full column rank,
\(G\) is symmetric positive definite. The mass-weighted least-squares
restriction from a full polar field to the reduced variables is

\[
R f
 = \left(
       G^{-1}V^T M_K f_K,\;
       f_O
     \right).
\]

The first component is the best mass-weighted Cartesian fit to the core
observations. The second component retains the outer polar values unchanged.
The associated prolongation is

\[
T(c,o) = (Vc,o).
\]

The reduced space is therefore

\[
Y = \mathbb{R}^{p}\oplus X_O.
\]

The reduced field is not an approximation with an additional independent
core field: its core values are exactly the values generated by \(Vc\).

## 3. Fundamental identities

### 3.1 Right-inverse identity

For \(y=(c,o)\in Y\),

\[
RTy
 = \left(
     G^{-1}V^T M_K(Vc),\;o
   \right)
 = (c,o)=y.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{RT=I_Y.}
\]

The reverse composition \(TR\) is the mass-weighted projector onto the
axis-regular core subspace and the identity on the outer space:

\[
TR(f_K,f_O)
 = \left(VG^{-1}V^TM_Kf_K,\;f_O\right).
\]

The polar core components in the nullspace of the fit are intentionally not
part of the reduced unknown space.

### 3.2 Reduced inner product and exact norm preservation

Define the reduced mass matrix and inner product by

\[
H = \begin{bmatrix}G&0\\0&M_O\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\langle (c,o),(d,e)\rangle_H
 = c^T Gd + o^T M_O e.
\]

For any \(y=(c,o)\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|Ty\|_M^2
 &= (Vc)^T M_K(Vc)+o^T M_Oo \\
 &= c^T V^TM_KVc+o^TM_Oo \\
 &= c^T Gc+o^TM_Oo \\
 &= \|y\|_H^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(T\) is an isometry from \((Y,H)\) into the full polar space with
the \(M\)-inner product:

\[
\boxed{\|Ty\|_M=\|y\|_H.}
\]

This is an exact identity for the discrete mass matrices, not an asymptotic
statement about the continuous coordinate system.

### 3.3 Adjoint identity

For every \(y=(c,o)\in Y\) and \(f=(f_K,f_O)\in X\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle Ty,f\rangle_M
 &= c^TV^TM_Kf_K+o^TM_Of_O \\
 &= c^TG\left(G^{-1}V^TM_Kf_K\right)+o^TM_Of_O \\
 &= \langle y,Rf\rangle_H.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore \(R\) is the \((M,H)\)-adjoint of \(T\):

\[
\boxed{T^T M = H R,\qquad R=H^{-1}T^TM.}
\]

This identity is the reason that the mass-weighted restriction must be used
in the reduced solve. An unweighted pseudoinverse generally does not give the
Galerkin adjoint or the exact norm relation above.

## 4. Reduced Galerkin elliptic problem

Let \(A:X\to X\) be the full materialized polar discretization of the
perpendicular elliptic operator, including its operator-specific boundary
flux closures. Let \(b\in X\) be the full right-hand side. The reduced
unknown \(y\in Y\) is defined by the Galerkin equation

\[
RATy = Rb.
\]

Using \(R=H^{-1}T^TM\), this is equivalent to

\[
H^{-1}T^T M A T y = H^{-1}T^T M b.
\]

Multiplying by \(H\) gives the standard weighted Galerkin system

\[
\boxed{T^T M A T y = T^T M b.}
\]

The reduced matrix is

\[
A_Y = RAT = H^{-1}T^T M A T,
\]

and the reduced right-hand side is

\[
b_Y=Rb=H^{-1}T^T M b.
\]

The reconstructed full solution is \(\phi=Ty\). It is axis regular by
construction in the core, with no independent polar degrees of freedom in
the discarded core complement.

If \(A\) is self-adjoint and positive definite in the full \(M\)-inner
product, then \(T^T M A T\) is self-adjoint and positive definite in the
reduced Euclidean coefficient representation. More generally, the Galerkin
formulation remains the correct restriction even when the full discretization
is nonsymmetric, provided the same operator and boundary closures are used
inside \(A T\).

## 5. Boundary lifting and physical nullspaces

The equations above describe a homogeneous reduced unknown. Inhomogeneous
boundary data must be represented by a lift before solving. Let \(\ell\in X\)
be a full polar field satisfying the prescribed physical boundary values and
axis-core compatibility. Write

\[
\phi = \ell + Ty.
\]

Then the reduced equation is

\[
T^T M A T y
 = T^T M(b-A\ell).
\]

The lift must be constructed with the same operator-level boundary semantics
as the production operator. In particular, a physical Dirichlet or Neumann
condition is not converted once into a universal field-level flux and then
reused for every conservative operator. Each operator's boundary closure
must be included consistently in the materialized action \(A(\ell+Ty)\).

For periodic or homogeneous Neumann problems, a physical constant nullspace
may remain. The constant function must be represented by the Cartesian basis;
this requires the constant monomial to be present in \(V\). Let \(\mathbf{1}
\in X\) denote the full constant vector and define its reduced coordinate

\[
h = R\mathbf{1},
\qquad T h=\mathbf{1}
\]

when the constant is exactly represented. The mass-weighted mean constraint
is

\[
\langle \mathbf{1},Ty\rangle_M
 = \mathbf{1}^TM Ty
 = h^T H y = 0.
\]

The compatibility condition for the right-hand side is

\[
\mathbf{1}^T M b=0,
\]

or, after boundary lifting,

\[
\mathbf{1}^T M(b-A\ell)=0.
\]

Implementation may enforce the gauge by fixing one reduced coefficient, by
adding a Lagrange multiplier, or by projecting the reduced Krylov vectors
onto the mass-mean-zero subspace. The choice must be made in reduced space;
it should not create an independent polar core gauge variable.

## 6. Residuals and acceptance criteria

For a reduced iterate \(y\), define the materialized full field

\[
\phi_y=Ty,
\qquad
r_X=b-A\phi_y.
\]

The Galerkin/reduced residual is

\[
r_Y=Rr_X
 = R(b-ATy).
\]

The natural acceptance norm for the reduced solve is

\[
\|r_Y\|_H
 = \left(r_Y^T H r_Y\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Equivalently, using the adjoint identity,

\[
\|r_Y\|_H^2
 = (T^TMr_X)^T H^{-1}(T^TMr_X).
\]

This is the residual of the equations tested against every admissible
axis-regular reduced basis function. It is the correct residual for accepting
the reduced Galerkin solve.

The full residual must nevertheless be reported separately. Its component
outside the reduced test space is

\[
r_{\mathrm{incomp}}
 = (I-TR)r_X.
\]

The full residual decomposes as

\[
r_X = TRr_X + (I-TR)r_X,
\]

where the first term is the tested/Galerkin component and the second term is
invisible to the reduced equations. Report, for example,

\[
\|r_Y\|_H,
\qquad
\|TRr_X\|_M,
\qquad
\|(I-TR)r_X\|_M,
\qquad
\|r_X\|_M.
\]

The word *incompatible* refers to a full RHS or full residual component that
cannot be represented or tested by the reduced axis-regular problem. A small
reduced residual does not prove that the full polar residual is small. Both
quantities are needed for diagnostics, especially during the transition from
the current full-grid solver.

## 7. Reduced preconditioning

Suppose \(B:X\to X\) is a full-space preconditioner, such as the current
radial line solve augmented with an axis-core treatment. Its mapped reduced
operator is

\[
\boxed{B_Y^{-1}=R B^{-1} T.}
\]

Applied to a reduced residual \(r_Y\), the action is:

\[
r_Y
\xrightarrow{T}
Tr_Y
\xrightarrow{B^{-1}}
B^{-1}Tr_Y
\xrightarrow{R}
RB^{-1}Tr_Y.
\]

This is the preconditioner that belongs to the reduced matrix \(A_Y=RAT\).
Applying the old full-grid preconditioner only to an expanded vector without
the final restriction would not produce a map on the reduced unknown space.

The first implementation can use the existing full operator and preconditioner
as black-box actions, with \(T\) and \(R\) wrapping them. This preserves the
operator's boundary closures and makes the reduced formulation a testing
option. It is not yet the most efficient implementation because every reduced
matvec materializes a full polar field.

Future alternatives include:

* a block preconditioner with a Cartesian-core block, an outer polar block,
  and an explicit core/outer transition block;
* a block factorization or Schur complement,
  \(\begin{bmatrix}A_{cc}&A_{co}\\A_{oc}&A_{oo}\end{bmatrix}\), with an
  approximate solve for
  \(A_{oo}-A_{oc}A_{cc}^{-1}A_{co}\);
* a periodic toroidal block solve for the core coefficients coupled to
  radial line solves in the outer region;
* a multilevel method whose coarsest axis representation is Cartesian while
  the bulk remains polar.

All of these alternatives should be compared against the mathematically
defined mapped action \(RB^{-1}T\), which serves as the correctness baseline.

## 8. Why this is not timestep projection

The reduced formulation is not a timestep projection of a full solution. It
does not allow arbitrary core modes to evolve and then delete them after an
RK stage. Instead, the unknown presented to the elliptic solver is always

\[
\phi=Ty,
\]

so the independent core coordinates are Cartesian coefficients from the
start. Every Krylov vector is a perturbation in \(Y\), and every materialized
Krylov field is axis regular in the core. The reduction changes the domain of
the elliptic linear problem, not the time integrator's state update rule.

The vorticity or source field may still be stored in a full polar space for
the time-dependent model. If its RHS contains a component incompatible with
the reduced phi solve, compute and report

\[
b_{\mathrm{incomp}}=(I-TR)b
\]

or, after boundary lifting,

\[
(I-TR)(b-A\ell).
\]

Track this quantity by timestep and by RK stage, together with its core-ring
and toroidal dependence. A growing incompatible component indicates that the
source/vorticity evolution or its axis stencil is generating data outside the
domain assumed by the elliptic solve. Silently projecting that source after
the fact would conceal the model inconsistency; it should instead trigger an
axis-regular formulation or compatible boundary/source treatment for that
evolution operator.

## 9. Implementation assumptions and sharding

The first testing implementation should make the following assumptions
explicit:

* The configured core observation rings are fully active. The matrix \(V\),
  the core mass \(M_K\), and the ordering of core values must refer to the
  same active-cell set.
* Radial sharding is initially unsupported if the core rings are split across
  devices. The core restriction and prolongation require all observations in
  each Cartesian fit to be available together. Until a distributed reduction
  is implemented, reject a radial sharding layout that cuts through \(K\).
* Theta sharding requires a gather or an equivalent distributed assembly of
  all poloidal observations in each core ring. A local theta slice is not a
  complete Cartesian fit.
* Eta sharding is structurally simpler because the fit is independent for
  each eta plane. Each device can restrict/prolong its owned eta planes, with
  the usual all-gather or halo communication only where the toroidal operator
  couples neighboring planes.
* The global Cartesian coefficient layout must be explicit. In particular,
  the coefficient dimension is not a polar cell dimension and must not be
  confused with the theta axis in a named sharding specification.
* Boundary data, volume weights, and operator actions must use the same
  materialized global geometry. A reduced solver must not fit a local subset
  with weights that differ from the global core mass matrix.

These constraints are intentionally conservative for the first option. A
future distributed implementation can replace gathers with all-reduces for
the coefficient restriction and with distributed assembly for \(T\), but it
must preserve \(RT=I\) and the weighted adjoint identity.

## 10. Verification gates and comparison metrics

The reduced option should not be considered validated until the following
gates pass.

### Algebraic gates

For random reduced vectors \(y\) and full vectors \(f\), verify to numerical
precision:

\[
RTy=y,
\qquad
\|Ty\|_M^2=y^THy,
\qquad
\langle Ty,f\rangle_M=\langle y,Rf\rangle_H.
\]

Verify that the reduced matvec assembled by wrappers agrees with explicit
materialization:

\[
\operatorname{matvec}_{Y}(y)=R(A(Ty)).
\]

Verify the preconditioner wrapper:

\[
\operatorname{prec}_{Y}(r)=R(B^{-1}(Tr)).
\]

### Manufactured-solution gates

Choose a reduced manufactured solution \(y_*\), form \(\phi_*=Ty_*\), and
set

\[
b=A\phi_*.
\]

The reduced solve must recover \(y_*\) up to solver tolerance, and the
materialized solution must satisfy \(\phi_*=Ty_*\). Repeat with a constant
nullspace and an imposed mean-zero gauge when applicable.

### Physical-solver gates

On the same geometry, RHS, boundary lift, initial guess, tolerance, restart,
and maximum iteration count, compare:

* the existing full-grid GMRES with its current preconditioner;
* the full-grid axis-core preconditioner;
* the reduced Cartesian-core GMRES with mapped preconditioning;
* an unpreconditioned reduced solve as a formulation diagnostic.

For every run record:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{iteration count}, & \text{convergence/rejection status},\\
\text{initial and final reduced residual}, & \text{final full residual},\\
\|(I-TR)r_X\|_M, & \|(I-TR)b\|_M,\\
\text{mean/gauge error}, & \text{operator and preconditioner timings},\\
\text{peak memory and communication cost}. &
\end{array}
\]

The decisive comparison is not only the final residual. The reduced
formulation should remove the high-dimensional core complement and therefore
improve the convergence history, reduce restart stagnation, and avoid the
near-null singular directions observed in the full polar formulation. A
reduced residual improvement accompanied by a growing incompatible full
residual is not a successful solve; it is evidence that the source or
boundary treatment is outside the elliptic domain.

### Resolution and sharding gates

Repeat the algebraic and manufactured tests at the target \(32^3\) resolution
and at at least one smaller resolution. Run the supported eta-sharded layout
and explicitly test that unsupported radial/partial-core sharding fails with
a clear diagnostic rather than silently fitting incomplete rings. Finally,
compare the reduced solution and residual diagnostics across equivalent
replicated and eta-sharded layouts.
